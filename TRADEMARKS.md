- Quote the name in articles, talks, comparisons and reviews.

- Publish a fork, a redistributed build, a hosted service, or a browser-store

  tell whose software they are running and who to hold responsible for it.
- Use the name or logo in a way that suggests this project endorses or
  maintains your product.
- Register the name, a confusingly similar name, or the logo as your own
  trademark, domain or store listing.

## Why it is separate from the licence

The AGPL is about the freedom to use, study, modify and share the *code*. A
trademark is about not being misled regarding *who made the thing you are
running*. Keeping them separate is the norm for open-source projects (Mozilla,
Linux, Docker, WordPress all do this) and costs downstream users nothing: every
freedom the AGPL grants you over the code stays intact.
